# STRATEGY — how the remaining problems relate, and what actually generalises

Companion to `FRAME_2026-09-23.md`. That file says WHERE WE ARE; this says WHAT TO DO and
WHY, and is meant to survive several compactions.

GOAL: the best possible DoD for elbaext, then expansion -- across Minnesota, and/or into
generalised algorithms. Those two expansions want different things, and the difference
decides several choices below.

---

## 1. THE ONE EQUATION THAT ORGANISES EVERYTHING

Every open problem is this:

    DoD_observed = (Z2_true + b2) - (Z1_true + b1) = dZ_true + (b2 - b1)

`b1`, `b2` are each epoch's near-ground VEGETATION BIAS -- how far its delivered ground
floats above the real surface. We want `dZ_true`; we observe it plus `(b2 - b1)`.

Everything we have measured is a statement about `b1` and `b2`:

* floodplain: `b1` is large (Nov 2008 standing dead sedge blocks gen1). DoD reads negative.
* hillslope: `b2` is large (May 2021 green-up blocks gen2). DoD reads positive.
* the two are CORRELATED at +0.630 across the tile, so they partly cancel and each hides
  the other. Marginal regressions see neither properly.

**THERE ARE ONLY TWO STRATEGIES, and they are not equally good.**

**(A) ESTIMATE EACH EPOCH'S GROUND WITHOUT BIAS.** Replace the biased estimator (a median,
which a vegetation tail drags up) with one that targets the ground population. Then `b1`
and `b2` both go to ~0 and `(b2 - b1)` vanishes. **This never requires knowing the other
epoch.**

**(B) MODEL THE DIFFERENCE.** Regress the DoD on vegetation proxies and subtract. This is
what the span LoD and the `ground_q` calibration do.

**(A) is strictly better and the reason is structural, not aesthetic.** Strategy B must
separate `b1` from `b2` using proxies correlated at +0.630 -- and we spent this session
discovering that it cannot: the "gen2 effect" moved from +46 to +54 to ~+20 mm depending on
which gen1 proxy did the controlling, and its apparent size grew fivefold in exactly the
strata where the control was weakest. Strategy A sidesteps the entire problem. **Every hour
spent on per-epoch estimators is worth more than an hour spent on cross-epoch corrections.**

This is also the answer to "how do we make sure we do not confound gen1 vs gen2": **stop
building things that could confound them.** A per-epoch estimator has no opportunity to.

## 2. THE FLOODPLAIN — and the asymmetry that decides it

The mode works on gen2 (it holds within 4 mm across a vegetation range that moves the
median 32 mm). **The question is whether it works on gen1, and the answer is probably no.**

A mode estimator needs enough returns to FIND a mode. From the resampling experiment, in
the high-vegetation band:

    n returns    hsm bias vs full density    hsm per-cell scatter
        16                 +19.7 mm                  56.4 mm
        32                 +11.8 mm                  40.5 mm
        64                  +7.0 mm                  30.0 mm

**gen1 has ~16 near-ground returns per 5 m cell.** At that count the mode is biased +19.7 mm
and scatters 56.4 mm in heavy vegetation -- it cannot reliably find gen1's ground where
gen1 is most blocked, which is exactly the floodplain.

So the floodplain splits into two different problems:

* **gen2's floodplain ground: FIXABLE.** ~227 near-ground returns per cell. Apply the mode.
* **gen1's floodplain ground: probably NOT FIXABLE at 5 m.** Too few returns, and 6,432
  cells have NO gen1 return reaching the gen2 surface at all -- there is no ground signal
  to recover, at any density, with any estimator.

**Three honest options for gen1, in order of preference:**

1. **COARSEN gen1's cell for the ground estimate only.** Pooling a 3x3 neighbourhood gives
   ~150 returns, enough for a mode. COST: it mixes real relief, so it must be done on the
   SLOPE-NORMAL residual (the pipeline already computes this), and it lowers the effective
   resolution of gen1's ground -- which is acceptable on a flat floodplain and NOT on a
   bank. Testable directly: does the coarsened mode reduce the DoD's dependence on gen1
   spread?
2. **MASK where gen1 has no ground signal.** This is what the span LoD already does and it
   is the honest answer if (1) fails. It is not a failure of method; a surface never
   observed cannot be differenced.
3. Do not attempt a per-cell gen1 vegetation CORRECTION. gen1 cannot characterise its own
   vegetation (16 returns/cell, 97.8% single-return), and any proxy must come from gen2 --
   the wrong epoch, and the confound of section 1.

**Prediction to state before testing (1):** if gen1's floodplain problem is sparsity, the
coarsened mode should cut the DoD-vs-gen1-spread slope (currently **-0.466 mm/mm**)
substantially. If the slope barely moves, the problem is not sparsity but genuine
non-penetration, and option (2) is the answer.

## 3. THE HILLSLOPES — fixable, and the easier of the two

Here the blocked epoch is gen2 (May green-up), and **gen2 is the one with the density**.
That is the whole difference: on hillslopes the epoch that needs mixture separation can
afford it.

**The work:** grid gen2's hillslope ground with the mode instead of `ground_q = 0.50`,
rebuild, and see whether the DoD's dependence on gen2 vegetation collapses. Currently, at
fixed gen1 vegetation, the DoD rises ~+55.7 mm from low to high gen2 vegetation on
hillslopes. **If the mode is doing its job, that number should fall toward zero.** That is
a sharp, falsifiable target and it does not depend on separating the epochs.

**UNTESTED AND IT MATTERS HERE MORE THAN ON THE FLOODPLAIN: the mode versus SLOPE.** A
steep cell's ground population is itself broad and asymmetric -- 5 m across a 25 deg slope
spans 2.3 m of real relief. The mode was validated against a VEGETATION tail, not against
relief. Test it on bare steep ground, where the answer is known (the mode and the median
must agree), before trusting it on vegetated steep ground.

## 4. ANTI-CONFOUND PROTOCOL — the discipline, stated so it can be checked

Earned the hard way this session; each line is a mistake that actually happened.

1. **Compare like with like, or do not compare.** Before quoting that A differs from B, run
   the SAME estimator on the SAME population and show it reproduces. Three numbers this
   session (10 mm / 2 mm / 48 mm for one quantity) were one phenomenon seen through three
   estimators, and I narrated the differences as physics.
2. **Change ONE thing at a time.** Estimator, or population, or covariate set. Never three
   and then a story.
3. **A level is GAUGE.** Remove one constant from each field on a stated population, then
   compare structure. Never a difference of medians.
4. **A file footprint is not a population.** Fitting inside whatever cells an artifact
   happens to cover produced a coefficient 60% wrong (`m = -0.294` from a 30% footprint).
5. **Would the algebra give this anyway?** If x and y share a term, derive the null before
   claiming physics. Resampling WITH replacement cannot move a quantile; an intercept at
   x=0 where x never reaches 0 is a prediction.
6. **Systematic and random are different currencies.** Per-cell scatter averages down as
   1/sqrt(N); a systematic does not. A 10 mm systematic beats 47 mm of noise over any area
   bigger than ~20 cells.
7. **An internal metric cannot adjudicate a fit against itself.** The correction surface
   wins on stable-cell NMAD because that is what it minimises.

## 5. WHAT GENERALISES — the criterion for Minnesota and for algorithms

The two expansions want different things, and this is the test to apply to any new piece of
method:

**GENERALISES** -- per-epoch, self-calibrating, physically motivated, no cross-epoch fitted
constant:
  * the star frame (each swath fitted independently against a reference)
  * a mode ground estimator (a property of a distribution, not of a site)
  * threshold-free physical tests (does any return reach the reference surface?)
  * the gauge discipline (a level is never an error)

**DOES NOT GENERALISE** -- anything whose value was fitted here:
  * `m = -0.466` mm/mm, the 450 mm failure floor, `valley_top_m = 230`, the 2 deg slope cut
  * any coefficient calibrated by forcing one landscape class to match another
  * the DeLong correction surface, which imprints its own shape at its IDW radius

**THE RULE: a method travels; a number does not.** For statewide work, every site-fitted
constant must either become a measured per-site quantity with a stated producer, or be
eliminated. The current count of site-fitted constants in the adopted path is small -- that
is the main thing that makes expansion plausible, and it should be kept small deliberately.

## 6. SEQUENCING — what to do in what order, and why

1. **Validate the mode against the control marks.** Cheap, external, and it is the only
   check that is not internal consistency. Open, well-observed ground is where a mode and a
   median MUST agree; disagreement there falsifies the estimator before it is built into
   anything. **Do this first; everything else is downstream of trusting the estimator.**
2. **Test the mode against slope** on bare steep ground (section 3).
3. **Apply the mode to gen2** and measure the hillslope target (+55.7 -> ?).
4. **Test coarsened-gen1** on the floodplain against the -0.466 slope (section 2).
5. Only then revisit the LoD, the 450 mm floor, and anything statewide.

Steps 1 and 2 are cheap and could invalidate 3-5. Do not reorder.
