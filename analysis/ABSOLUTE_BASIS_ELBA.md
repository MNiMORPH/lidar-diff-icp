# An absolute elevation basis for Elba, and what it is worth

**Date:** 2026-08-26
**Scripts:** `analysis/groundtruth/reference_swath_bias.py` (the transport question),
`analysis/groundtruth/gen2_checkpoint_tie.py` (gen2 against its own marks),
`analysis/groundtruth/elba_datum_constant.py` (the constant, its budget, the product),
`analysis/groundtruth/elba_absolute_tie.py --json` (the gen1 ties, re-run today)
**Module:** `src/lidar_diff_icp/groundtruth/datum.py`, tested in
`tests/test_groundtruth_datum.py`
**Product:** `data/derived/elba_fulldensity/z_before_absolute.npy` +
`z_before_absolute.json`

Every table below is generated by the script that computed it, through
`trust/provenance.py`, so each column carries its own definition and each parameter names
its source. Nothing in `pipeline.py`, `coreg.py` or `references.py` was changed; they are
imported.

**Sign convention.** The datum constant is what you **ADD to gen1**:
`tie = surveyed − z_lidar_corrected`. Raising gen1 therefore **lowers** the DoD, which is
`gen2 − gen1`.

## 1. The result

**gen1 at Elba reads +22.7 mm low against surveyed NAVD88(GEOID18).**

Two independent chains carry the tie in from opposite sides of the site. The west chain
runs 128-129-130-131-132-133 from mark 2210, five links, and lands on swath 133, which is
elbaext's own gauge swath. The east chain runs 144-143-142-141-140-139-138 from mark 2036,
six links, and lands on swath 138, which then takes elbaext's internal alignment into 133's
frame. They share no overlap, no tile and no mark.

| mark | type | tie (mm) | σ (mm) | links | ΔN from band (km) | role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2210 | NVA | **+21.3** | 12.4 | 5 | −0.33 | anchor (west) |
| 2036 | NVA | **+28.9** | 27.0 | 6 | −0.21 | anchor (east) |
| 3056 | VVA | −103.2 | 52.3 | 5 | −0.21 | not an anchor: VVA, vegetation in the ground class |
| 2024 | NVA | +156.6 | 54.5 | 4 | +3.24 | not an anchor: 3.24 km off band, ~50 mm of unmodelled drift |
| 2099 | NVA | – | – | – | – | not attempted: tile `4342-26-61` not on disk |
| 3089 | VVA | – | – | – | – | not attempted: tile `4358-26-02` not on disk |

The two anchors agree to **7.5 mm**. That is smaller than either mark's own σ, so it is a
consistency check and not the accuracy. Both exclusions are strata the method declared
before any tie was computed – 3DEP's own accuracy class, and the corridor band the links
were solved in – and both are arguments (`--anchor-types`, `--band-km`) that print their
source.

### The uncertainty budget

Each term is **measured in the run that reports it**, not quoted from elsewhere. Terms
carry a kind, because a term shared by every tie does not average down with the number of
marks.

| term | kind | mm | applies to | measured how |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| chain, per-link alignment | random | 19.8 | each anchor's own chain | the two Elba products align the same four swaths from different extents: 12.4 mm RMS over 1–3 links, **8.4 mm per link**, propagated over 5 and 6 links (18.8, 20.6 mm) |
| estimator + mark scatter | random | 40.8 | each mark | gen2 against four open-ground marks with **no** chain, geoid or lateral term: 40.8 mm RMS (§3). An upper bound; it cannot be separated from real gen2 spatial error |
| elbaext swath alignment, extent-dependence | common | 17.4 | the east tie, via swath 138 | worst-swath disagreement between the two products' alignments. Conservative: the west chain ends on the gauge swath and carries none of it |
| lateral (Nuth & Kääb) shift, extrapolated | common | 10.0 | all ties | applying and withholding the elbaext shift (−0.7498, −0.1893) m moves the anchors by −6.8 and +12.3 mm |
| ground source, CSF against vendor class 2 | common | 4.3 | all ties | re-reading the anchors with the other source moves them by +5.9 and +1.4 mm |
| horizontal datum, EPSG:6344 against EPSG:26915 | common | 0.0 | all ties | pyproj returns a null transform at all six marks. The NAD83(1986)→NAD83(2011) realization difference is **not** modelled by PROJ here and is therefore not in this number |
| -- **random subtotal** (after inverse-variance weighting) | random | **34.0** | 2 ties | includes each mark's own radius σ through the weights |
| -- **common subtotal** | common | **20.5** | all ties | independent of the number of ties |
| **σ_total** | | **39.7** | the constant | quadrature of the two subtotals |
| along-track drift, not applied | unmodelled | 5.1 | both anchors | 15.5 mm/km measured on the elbaext swaths; the anchors sit ≤0.33 km off band |
| validity of the lateral shift 7–16 km away | unmodelled | 42.6 | all ties | its measured effect reaches 42.6 mm across the four attempted marks; whether it holds out there is an assumption |

The two unmodelled rows are bounds, not σ, and they are deliberately kept **out** of
`σ_total`. Folding a knowledge gap into a quadrature sum dresses it up as a measurement.

**The headline is therefore +22.7 ± 39.7 mm, with a 42.6 mm bound beside it.** The budget
is five times the agreement of the two chains, and §3 shows why that is the honest reading
rather than pessimism: four marks of the same accuracy class span 103 mm even when every
transport term is removed.

`combine_ties` weights the anchors by their own radius σ and refuses to shrink a
common-mode term with *n*. The regression test adds marks and asserts the common subtotal
does not move; it fails when `common_mm` is reverted to divide by the tie count.

## 2. The reference swath: a gauge, not an observation

Both tiles pin a swath the tile clips. elba's swath 135 has a mean signed scan angle of
**−11.79°** over the tile and elbaext's swath 133 has **−12.14°**, while the interior
swaths sit between **+0.13° and +1.77°**. The worry was that a per-swath "constant" is
really an across-track function averaged over whatever scan angles the tile samples, so
every constant is measured against a biased reference.

### The transport premise is false, and that is the first result

`coreg.align_swaths` solves a **free-network** least squares over every pairwise
observation and only then subtracts the reference swath's value (`coreg.py:485`:
`c -= c[idx[ref]]`). The reference is applied **after** the solve. Re-referencing is
therefore arithmetic: it shifts every constant by one number and leaves every *difference*
untouched.

Demonstrated on a synthetic four-strip cloud whose outermost strip is cut in half, the
geometry of the real tiles:

| ref | largest change in any swath-to-swath difference | change in absolute level |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 3.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ mm | −14.7 mm |
| 1 | 0 | −15.8 mm |
| 2 | 0 | +8.4 mm |
| 3 | 3.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ mm | +22.2 mm |

**Re-solving the internal alignment with a symmetrically-sampled interior swath as
reference cannot collapse the elba/elbaext disagreement, and cannot change what a tie
transports.** The disagreement lives in the differences, and the gauge does not touch them.
`tests/test_coreg.py::test_align_swaths_reference_is_a_gauge_not_an_observation` pins this;
it fails when the network is restricted to the edges incident on the reference, which is
exactly the "everything measured against the reference" the worry describes.

### The physical worry is real one level down

The edge observations are where an across-track term can act, because
`coregister_swaths(a, b)` reads each pair on the cells the two swaths **share**. What
matters is not a swath's mean scan angle over the tile but the angles each member presents
inside that strip:

| tile | edge | shared cells | scan a | scan b | asymmetry |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| elba | 135-136 | 40,476 | −11.92° | −8.44° | **+3.47°** |
| elba | 136-137 | 62,985 | +10.65° | +10.74° | +0.08° |
| elba | 137-138 | 64,301 | −9.93° | −10.49° | −0.56° |
| elbaext | 133-134 | 44,805 | −12.27° | −8.30° | **+3.97°** |
| elbaext | 134-135 | 70,239 | +10.74° | +10.95° | +0.21° |
| elbaext | 135-136 | 74,107 | −10.07° | −10.24° | −0.17° |
| elbaext | 136-137 | 73,499 | +10.70° | +10.75° | +0.05° |
| elbaext | 137-138 | 76,293 | −9.82° | −10.57° | −0.75° |

This corrects the worry rather than confirming it. In a strip **both** members sit 10–12°
off nadir, because a strip is by construction the far edge of one swath and the far edge of
the next. The asymmetry that can bias an edge runs 0.05–0.75° on every interior edge and
+3.5 to +4.0° on the **outermost edge alone** – the one whose lower member is the
half-swath the tile clips.

### The controlled test, one term changed

`boresight.estimate_boresight`, run identically on both tiles' own per-return angle tables,
measures the across-track slope at **+2.19 mm/deg** (elba) and **+1.80 mm/deg** (elbaext),
with per-pair standard deviations of 0.71 and 0.82. The two tiles measure it independently
and agree.

That estimator yields two vertical registrations from the *same* cells: the between-line
mean, which carries the across-track term, and the intercept of the same data regressed on
the between-line scan-angle difference, which does not. Chaining each into per-swath
constants and differencing the two tiles isolates the term, because nothing else changes.

| variant | swath 136 | 137 | 138 | RMS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pipeline `align_swaths`, as saved | +8.0 | +9.8 | +17.4 | **12.4 mm** |
| same estimator, across-track term **in** | +7.1 | +10.9 | +9.6 | **9.3 mm** |
| same estimator, across-track term **out** | +3.2 | +6.8 | +4.6 | **5.1 mm** |

Two results. First, an independent estimator reproduces the anomaly at 9.3 mm against the
pipeline's 12.4 mm, so it is a property of the data and not of `coregister_swaths`. Second,
removing only the across-track term takes it from 9.3 to 5.1 mm RMS, a quadrature reduction
of 7.8 mm. **The across-track sampling accounts for roughly half of the disagreement; it
does not abolish it.** The 5.1 mm that survives is extent-dependence for other reasons,
principally that the two overlaps sample different terrain.

**Reported either way, as asked.** The gauge test does not collapse anything, and it cannot;
the edge-sampling test does collapse about half. The practical consequence for the datum is
that the per-link chain error is 8.4 mm rather than the ~5 mm it would be if the across-track
term were corrected, which is the difference between an 18.8 mm and an 8.2 mm chain term
over five links.

## 3. gen2 against its own checkpoints

The six marks are the 2021 3DEP project's own vertical-accuracy points, surveyed in
NAVD88(GEOID18), the same datum as gen2. gen2 can therefore be read against them with
**every transport term removed**: no chain, no geoid shift, no lateral extrapolation. The
estimator and the radius ladder are the ones the gen1 tie uses, so the two epochs are
comparable rather than merely both present. This had never been run;
`docs/groundtruth.md` §7 listed it as a gap.

Six 400 m boxes were fetched at full density (EPT depth 12, 3.4–8.7 ground points per m²,
57 MB) by `scripts/fetch_3dep_checkpoint_boxes.sh`.

| mark | type | n at R = 7.5 m | gen2 tie (mm) | σ (mm) | median over radii (mm) | inside 3DEP's own class accuracy? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2210 | NVA | 1,293 | **−0.7** | 11.5 | +18.3 | yes |
| 3056 | VVA | 1,569 | **+27.9** | 20.7 | +18.9 | yes |
| 2024 | NVA | 1,334 | **+35.1** | 39.4 | +28.7 | **no** (radius spread 78.9 mm against 35 mm) |
| 2036 | NVA | 1,157 | **+59.4** | 27.4 | +13.8 | **no** (radius spread 54.9 mm against 35 mm) |
| 2099 | NVA | 927 | **−43.7** | 8.7 | −49.3 | yes |
| 3089 | VVA | 803 | **−109.2** | 36.9 | −94.8 | yes |

A positive tie means gen2 sits **low** of the survey. Re-reading each mark with CSF instead
of vendor class 2 moves it by −8.5 to +2.5 mm, so the ground source does not carry these
numbers.

**Two findings, and the second is the harder one.**

(1) **gen2 is not systematically low.** Over the four open-ground marks the median is
**+17.2 mm**, and +16.1 mm when each mark is read as the median over the pipeline-scale
radii instead of at R = 7.5 m. The measurement also confirms the datum assumption it was
run under: gen2 on a different geoid model would show a ~70 mm offset at every mark, and it
does not. The gen1 constant of +22.7 mm is therefore gen1's own and not an artifact shared
by both epochs.

(2) **The mark and its terrain, not the lidar, set the floor.** This is the cleanest case
the method has – full density, its own datum, nothing transported – and the four
open-ground marks still span **103 mm** and give **40.8 mm RMS**. Two of the four fail
3DEP's own 35 mm RMSEz on radius spread alone. Whatever makes a surveyed monument disagree
with the lidar ground at that size – siting on a road crown, the monument against the
surface, the survey's own target definition – is a property of the mark, and it applies just
as much when gen1 is read there. That is why it enters the gen1 budget at 40.8 mm and why
it dominates it.

### The epoch difference

gen1's tie already carries the GEOID03→GEOID18 shift and the chain into the elbaext swath
frame, so `gen1 tie − gen2 tie` is the epoch residual **after** the geoid term, for the gen1
surface as the product uses it.

| mark | gen1 − gen2 (mm) |
|---|---|
| 2210 | +22.0 |
| 2024 | +121.5 |
| 2036 | −30.5 |

Median +22.0 mm over three open-ground marks. The leveled benchmark DG8385 put the same
residual at **+5 mm with a std of 11 mm**
(`analysis/ridgelines/FLAGPOLE_ABSOLUTE_TEST.md`), and it remains the tighter measurement:
it is one mark read many times over a ±3 m position grid, where these are three marks read
once each through the scatter documented above. The checkpoints do not contradict the
benchmark, and they do not sharpen it.

## 4. The product

`data/derived/elba_fulldensity/z_before_absolute.npy` is the gen1 bare-earth surface on the
surveyed datum: 700 × 508 cells at 5 m, 341,240 finite, EPSG:26915, built as

```
z_before_absolute = (z_after − dod) + 22.665 mm
```

`pipeline.py` writes `dod = Zref − Z08c`, so `z_after − dod` **is** the registered gen1
grid; no new estimator is introduced and the geoid-only datum the product was built on is
untouched beneath it. The sidecar `z_before_absolute.json` records the constant, the sign
convention, both ties with their chain paths, the full budget with every term's source, the
marks that do **not** anchor and why, gen2's offsets at all six marks, and the implied DoD
shift. The script refuses to overwrite either file, and **`dod.npy` is not modified**.

The DoD on this basis is `dod.npy − 22.7 mm` everywhere. That subtraction is deliberately
left to the caller rather than baked into a second DoD array, so there is exactly one DoD
on disk and one recorded constant that moves it.

## 5. What the DoD gains, and what it does not

**Gains: the level.** The DoD's absolute datum was free. It is now pinned at +22.7 mm with
a stated budget, both epochs are checked against the same surveyed marks, and the
GEOID03→GEOID18 term is no longer the only thing standing between the two epochs. A volume
integrated over the tile now has a defensible zero, and its systematic component is
39.7 mm × area rather than unknown × area.

**Does not gain: the shape.** The spatially varying residual is a separate error term and a
constant removes none of it. Measured today on `elba_fulldensity`, on the stable divide
cells `refcells.reference_cells` already trusts (57,056 cells surviving the divide,
curvature, slope, building, clear-cut, bluff-margin and gross-change cuts), in blocks of
500 m:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| blocks with ≥2 stable cells | 42 |
| median stable cells per block | 1,470 |
| per-block median DoD, range | **−107 to +50 mm** |
| between-block sd, observed | 33.1 mm |
| within-block sampling noise | 9.6 mm |
| **between-block sd, real** | **31.7 mm** |

The sampling-noise correction matters: without it, thin blocks manufacture spatial
structure. What survives is real. A 31.7 mm block-to-block wander is the same size as the
datum constant itself, it varies across the tile, and **the datum fixes the level while this
sets the shape** – only the first is what a ground-control tie can reach.

## 6. What remains unconstrained

1. **Two marks were never tried.** 2099 and 3089 need gen1 tiles `4342-26-61` and
   `4358-26-02`. 2099 sits on line 130, the same western chain, 9 km north; it would add a
   third path but would inherit 2024's along-track problem.
2. **The lateral shift is still extrapolated.** It was measured against gen2 at Elba and
   applied 3.1–15.6 km away. Its effect is reported per mark and reaches 42.6 mm, but its
   validity out at line 128 is an assumption, and it is the largest single unmodelled term.
3. **Along-track drift is still not applied at the marks.** `fit_along_track_drift`
   regresses against the gen2 grid, which does not exist there. The anchors sit within
   0.33 km of the band, so the term is ≤5 mm for them; it is 50 mm for 2024 and it is why
   2024 does not anchor.
4. **The 5.1 mm that the across-track correction leaves.** Half the elba/elbaext
   disagreement is across-track sampling and half is not. The remainder tracks which terrain
   each overlap happens to contain, and no correction proposed here reaches it.
5. **The estimator-and-mark floor.** 40.8 mm RMS on the cleanest possible case is the single
   largest term in the budget, and it will not fall by adding chains or correcting swaths.
   It falls only with more marks, or with marks sited somewhere other than local topographic
   highs. Four more anchors would take it from 28.8 mm to 16.7 mm in the combination; the survey
   provides six marks and two of them anchor today.
6. **The block-to-block residual.** 31.7 mm of real spatial structure on stable ground,
   untouched by anything in this document.

### Proposed integration, not performed

`pipeline.difference_dem` should take an optional `datum_constant_m` that it adds to the
gen1 grid and records in `corrections.json` beside `cross_epoch_datum`, with the tie
provenance carried through. That keeps one DoD on disk and makes the constant a first-class
term rather than a subtraction the reader has to remember. It is not implemented here,
because `pipeline.py` was out of scope for this work.
