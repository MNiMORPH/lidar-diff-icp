## CONVENTIONS — get these right, they were repeatedly confused this session

### 1. Signs. Two opposite conventions are in play.
- **`d_mm` / `d_mm_corr`** (per-return, per-beam table) = `(z_gen1 - plane_gen2(x,y)) / |n| * 1000`
  (`gen1_save_angles_slope.py:71`). **POSITIVE = gen1 above gen2 = ground LOWER in 2021.**
- **`dod.npy`** (gridded) = `Zref - Z08c` = gen2 - gen1 (`pipeline.py:732`).
  **POSITIVE = gen2 above gen1 = elevation ROSE = deposition.** README: red = erosion, blue = deposition.
- They are opposite. Verified empirically on 335,194 cells: `DoD = -0.874 * d_mm_corr + 0.52`,
  r = -0.879 (not exactly -1: different ground estimators, same sign relation).
- Figure axes now state it: `offset d (mm) = gen1 - gen2 (+ = ground lower in 2021)`.

### 2. Slope-normal. What it is and what it does NOT mean.
- `|n| = sqrt(gx^2 + gy^2 + 1) = sec(slope)`; dividing a vertical difference by `|n|` gives the
  distance PERPENDICULAR to the gen2 ground plane.
- The plane is subtracted at the point's OWN (x,y): `z - (z_cell + gx*dx + gy*dy)`. Subtracting
  only `z_cell` leaves intra-cell relief `+-3.54 m * tan(slope)` (= +-2 m at 30 deg). That was
  the cube bug.
- **Slope-normal removes terrain, so slope is NOT a variable in anything built on it.** After
  registration the offset is FLAT vs incidence 0-45 deg and flat vs slope at fixed cover.
  Any slope-band table is a cover gradient in disguise (median cover rises 0.000 -> 0.343
  across slope bands 0-6 -> 30-36 deg). Do not stratify by slope.
- Low vegetation is compressed by cos(slope) in this frame (13% at 30 deg, ~4 cm on a 30 cm
  plant) and small plants grow surface-perpendicular anyway, so slope-normal is the right
  single frame for everything here.
- GOTCHA: `slope.npy` is `degrees(slope_aspect(gaussian_filter(Zf, 2.0), res))` -- a SMOOTHED
  gradient (sigma = 2 cells = 10 m). The cube's plane uses `np.gradient(zf, RES)` on the
  UNSMOOTHED surface. Close but not identical; do not treat them as the same array.

### 3. Vegetation quantities -- four different things, do not interchange.
- **`canopy_cover_pfs.npy`** -- PyForestScan cover, plant-area based, **min_height = 2.0 m**,
  from the gen2 (2021) cloud only. This is "cover" everywhere above. It CANNOT see anything
  below 2 m.
- **`gen{1,2}_canopy_frac.npz["frac"]`** -- fraction of THAT EPOCH's own returns >2 m above
  z_after. Identical definition for both epochs, so 2008-vs-2021 canopy is comparable.
  (`n_ret`, `n_above`, `n_bldg`, `frac` -- use `frac`, not `n_ret`.)
- **`lowveg_frac.npy`** -- 0.5-2 m band from the 2021 cloud. **elba only.** It behaves as a
  PENETRATION indicator (returns there mean pulses got below 2 m), not a vegetation density.
- **The near-ground column** (`nearground_cells_sn.npz`) -- ALL returns within -1..+2 m
  slope-normal. This is the only object that sees below 0.5 m, i.e. the herb layer.
- Vegetation below ~0.3-0.5 m is NOT separable return-by-return from ground; it shows up as
  a lifted/skewed upper part of the near-ground distribution, not as a distinct band. The
  literature's understory strata all start at 0.5 m for this reason.

### 4. Percentile RANK is not elevation.
`q1 - q2 = +0.18` means gen1's ground sits at a higher RANK within gen1's own column than
gen2's does within gen2's. In ELEVATION gen1's ground is mostly BELOW gen2's. Both hold at once.

### 5. Point density vs significance.
- **n enters ONCE, through the standard error.** Discounting a regime a second time for being
  rare double-counts it. Rarity is a fact about the landscape, not about the measurement.
- **Returns/cells in one woodlot are not independent.** Return-counted SEs are **3.4-3.5x too
  small** here. Use `binstats.binned_stats(..., block=block_ids(...))`, which reports both and
  prefers the cluster-robust one. Effective n = number of 50 m blocks, not returns.
- Sparse high-cover bins are individually significant (e.g. -100.8 +- 21.2 mm, 53 cells,
  4.8 sigma). Do not truncate the covariate range; plot bins at their true span with counts.
- A minimum-returns-per-cell filter does NOT filter quality -- it re-selects toward open
  canopy (median cover falls 0.61 -> 0.53 across return-count bins).
- Per-cell noise floor is ~48 mm median / ~108-122 mm RMS on no-change ground; the cover
  effect (-5 to -60 mm) lives BELOW it per cell and only appears in medians over 1000s of cells.

## WHY THE BEST-FITTING PERCENTILES ARE AT OR BELOW THE MEDIAN (measured 2026-08-26)
`analysis/ridgelines/nearground_rank.py` — no fitting anywhere.

**1. The algebra.** The near-ground window has a hard floor and an open ceiling: nothing
scatters from below the ground, so every non-ground return sits ABOVE the surface.
Write the column as a ground kernel (weight `w_g`, roughly symmetric about the surface)
plus vegetation (weight `1-w_g`, entirely above it):

    F(ground) = w_g * 0.5 + (1 - w_g) * 0 = 0.5 * w_g   <=  0.5

The rank of the ground is **half the ground fraction of the window**. 0.5 is the ceiling,
reached only when the window holds nothing but ground. Contamination is one-sided-up, so
it can only push the rank DOWN. Nothing in the physics can push it up except mass genuinely
below the surface (ranging noise, timewalk on weak returns, multipath blunders).

**2. The measured ranks** (rank of each epoch's own ground in its own column; gen2's ground
is `h=0` by construction, gen1's is `h=-reg` with `reg` the per-cell registration correction
from `beam_offset_table.parquet`, median +55.0 / +75.2 mm):

| stratum | F1 elba | F2 elba | F1-F2 | F1 elbaext | F2 elbaext | F1-F2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| open <0.05    | 0.50 | 0.41 | +0.09 | 0.50 | 0.44 | +0.06 |
| light .05-.20 | 0.50 | 0.37 | +0.13 | 0.52 | 0.38 | +0.14 |
| mid .20-.35   | 0.53 | 0.38 | +0.15 | 0.54 | 0.38 | +0.16 |
| dense >0.35   | 0.54 | 0.38 | +0.16 | 0.53 | 0.38 | +0.15 |

gen2 carries **19-20% of its window above ground+0.10 m and 5% above +0.50 m**; the algebra
then predicts F2 ~ 0.5*0.80 = 0.40 and 0.38 is observed. gen1 carries 14-18% above +0.10 m
but **0.00 above +0.50 m** (Nov leaf-off: no shrub layer), and it has a much heavier LOW
tail — 27% of gen1's window lies more than 0.10 m BELOW its own ground vs 11% for gen2 —
so gen1's understory mass is nearly cancelled and its ground sits AT its own median.

**This reproduces the fitted q1-q2 = +0.12..+0.20 with no fitting**: +0.13/+0.11 pooled,
+0.15/+0.16 at mid cover.

**CORRECTION (same day): the rank gap is NOT the vegetation above the ground.** An
additive decomposition (mean per-cell fractions, stable cells, all slopes) puts the whole
gap in the SUB-GROUND tail:

| band about each epoch's own ground | gen1 mid cover | gen2 mid cover | gen1 open | gen2 open |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| below -0.10 m | **0.25 / 0.26** | **0.10 / 0.10** | **0.11 / 0.10** | **0.02 / 0.02** |
| -0.10 .. 0    | 0.27 / 0.27 | 0.29 / 0.29 | 0.40 / 0.41 | 0.41 / 0.43 |
| 0 .. +0.10 m  | 0.24 / 0.24 | 0.35 / 0.35 | 0.36 / 0.38 | 0.54 / 0.53 |
| above +0.10 m | 0.23 / 0.24 | 0.26 / 0.26 | 0.13 / 0.11 | 0.03 / 0.03 |

(elba / elbaext.) Above +0.10 m the two epochs are the SAME under canopy (0.23-0.26 both),
so understory mass cannot be the driver. The gap is entirely `below -0.10 m`: gen1 puts
2-5x more of its near-ground returns well BELOW the ground than gen2 does, in every
stratum **including bare open ground where there is no vegetation at all** (0.11 vs 0.02,
gap +0.09 = the whole open rank gap).

Nothing is physically below the ground, so that mass is gen1 measurement error -- the 2008
sensor's blunder/multipath population and broader ranging distribution (gen1 sigma 17 mm
vs gen2 11 mm; gen1 IQR 1.5-2x gen2). It is what CSF+ELM classifies away; the all-return
column keeps it. **gen2's column sits ON the ground with a hard floor; gen1's STRADDLES
it.** That is why gen2's ground is at rank 0.38 and gen1's at 0.52.

NOT a swath-mixing artifact of using one mean `reg` per cell: single-swath cells
(within-cell reg sd = 0.0 mm) show the same or a slightly LARGER low tail than 2-swath
cells (0.204 vs 0.196 elba, 0.189 vs 0.175 elbaext; open 0.106 vs 0.105).

**3. The objective is a VALLEY, not a point.** Mapping med|Q1(q1)-Q2(q2)+reg| over the full
(q1,q2) grid (elba, cover .20-.35, 33,378 cells): minimum 53.3 mm at (0.34, 0.18); every
pair within 2 mm of it lies on a ridge of constant difference, **mean q1-q2 = +0.170**,
spanning q1 = 0.18-0.58. Along the valley the objective is flat to ~1 mm up to q1 ~ 0.58,
then degrades: 56.3 at q1=0.64, 81.6 at 0.90, 106.2 at 0.96.

So the fit pins the **difference**, not the absolute levels — do not over-read a reported
q1 = 0.26. And the argmin lands at or below the median because the lower flank of both
columns is pinned to the same hard physical floor, while the upper flank is vegetation
whose amount varies cell to cell. Matching high in the column matches noise; matching low
matches the ground.
